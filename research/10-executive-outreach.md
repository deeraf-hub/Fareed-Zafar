# Executive outreach: 19 messages to named decision makers

Written for rows in `09-executive-target-list.md`. Every message below goes to a founder, CEO, CMO or CRO by name.

---

## Why these read nothing like the earlier ones

The messages in `07-hsf-outreach-messages.md` were applications. They mentioned rate, availability and portfolio, because the reader had already decided they needed a designer and was choosing between candidates.

An executive has decided nothing. Sending them rate and availability is like handing a CFO your CV when they asked about their margin. So four things drop out entirely:

**No rate.** Price comes up on the call, after they want it. Naming a number first makes you a line item.

**No availability.** "I can start Monday" tells a CEO you have nothing on. Say nothing about your calendar.

**No portfolio link in message one.** A link is a task. You are asking a busy person to do homework before they have any reason to. Earn the link with the reply.

**No description of your services.** They can work out what a deck designer does. What they cannot work out on their own is why it matters to them this quarter.

What replaces all of it is one observation about **their** business, specific enough that it could not have been sent to anybody else, followed by a question they can answer in about four seconds.

## The shape every message follows

1. One line naming the thing that just happened to them, in their language.
2. One line naming the consequence they have probably not thought about yet.
3. One line that makes clear you can fix that, without listing how.
4. A question that takes four seconds.

That is four lines. Executives read four lines. They do not read seven.

## The two formats

**Connection note**, under 300 characters, which is the LinkedIn limit. This is the default and the one you will use most.

**Follow-up message**, sent three to five days after they accept, longer, still short. One new idea, never a repeat of the first message with more words.

Character counts are given so you can check nothing drifted if you edit.

## Before you send

- **Confirm the person is still in the seat.** Every name here came from a press release, not a profile. Executives move.
- **Never mention a job posting**, even where one exists and you know about it.
- **Germany, Austria and Italy are opt-in jurisdictions.** LinkedIn only for Enzo and ViteSicure, never cold email.
- **Turn off open and click tracking** for anyone in the UK or EU.

# How to work this list without burning it

**Week one, the five best.** Shroff at Arintra, Barak at MIND, Weiss at Luzern Risk, Liametov at Skyro, Pearson at Financial Information Technologies. All five raised or changed something inside the last five weeks, all five are reachable, and all five have a problem you can name in one line.

**Week two, the rest of section A.** Saari, Patel, Freckleton, Liu, Ortega. Every one of these is a founder in the one-to-two-quarter window after a raise.

**Week three, the larger fish.** Kopp, Malhotra, von Maltzahn, Brownsell, Volanoski. Bigger companies, slower replies, worth the wait but not worth going first.

**Week four, the unnamed rows.** Spend that week finding the missing 18 names in `09-executive-target-list.md` rather than sending anything. Funding press releases name CEOs reliably, so this is about five minutes per company, and a named message beats an unnamed one by a margin that dwarfs everything else in this document.

## Three ways this goes wrong

**Sending to the wrong seat.** A seed founder reads your message personally. A Series D CMO does not. The stage table in `09-executive-target-list.md` exists for exactly this, and getting it wrong wastes a good message on a real prospect.

**Leading with yourself.** Every message above spends its first line on their business and none of them on your service. The moment a message opens with what you do, it becomes one of forty identical ones in that inbox.

**Following up with the same message.** A follow-up that restates the first one in more words tells them the first one was all you had. Every follow-up above introduces one genuinely new idea, which is the only kind worth sending.
