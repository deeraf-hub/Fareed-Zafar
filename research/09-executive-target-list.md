# Executive target list: healthcare, SaaS and fintech

Researched 18 September 2026. Built to be pitched, not applied to.

---

## What changed, and why

The earlier list in `06-healthcare-saas-fintech.md` used live freelance job postings as its evidence. That was the wrong instrument for what you actually want to do. A job posting routes you into a hiring pipeline as a candidate, where you are compared against other applicants on rate and availability. It is the same error we already identified on the 24Slides brief: a bench application arriving on a chief executive's desk.

This list uses a different signal. Every row is a company with a **business event that created a storytelling problem the executive personally owns**, and wherever it could be verified, that executive is named.

Three practical consequences of the switch:

1. **Never mention a job posting.** Even where one exists, it is your private intelligence that the company buys outside design. Saying it out loud turns you from a supplier into an applicant.
2. **The agency rows are gone.** Med comms agencies and design studios are a white-label play, not an executive pitch. They are still real revenue and they are still in `06`, Tier 2. Different motion, different conversation, do not mix the two.
3. **Fewer rows, deeper research.** 38 companies and 3 events, instead of 82 rows. You are pitching executives one at a time, so one name with a dated event and a known problem beats a long list of logos.

---

## Who to pitch, decided by company stage

Getting this wrong wastes the message even when the company is right.

| Company stage | Pitch this person | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Seed to Series A | **Founder or CEO** | There is no marketing department. The founder *is* the marketing department, still writes the decks personally, and can say yes without asking anyone. |
| Series B to C | **CMO, VP Marketing or Head of Brand** | A marketing leader now exists, owns a budget, and is measured on output they cannot yet produce fast enough. |
| Series C and later, or public | **CRO, VP Sales Enablement, or Head of IR** | Brand is staffed. The unmet need moves to sales collateral and investor materials, which sit outside the brand team. |
| Mid-rebrand, any stage | **Whoever is quoted in the rebrand press release** | They own the rollout publicly. Their name is in the announcement, which means the collateral gap is theirs to explain. |
| PE-backed or post-MBO | **CEO, and the Chair if newly appointed** | New investors arrive with a mandate to look more professional. That mandate lands on the CEO in the first year. |

One rule worth more than the table: **the smaller the company, the higher up you go.** A seed-stage founder will read your message. A Series D CMO will not.

---

## Odds column, read it before you write

| Grade | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Good** | Founder-led or small marketing team, recent money, and a specific problem you can name. Worth a genuinely tailored message. |
| **Fair** | Right event, but either a marketing team already exists or the company is big enough to have agency relationships. Worth one message, not three. |
| **Long** | Large, well-resourced, or already using a named agency. On the list because the event is real, not because the odds are. Send last, if at all. |

# B. Rebrand or repositioning in flight

The most underpitched signal in this business. The identity launch is the press release. The eighteen months of sales decks, investor updates, one-pagers, trade-show panels and town-hall slides still carrying the old logo is the work, and nobody budgets for it because nobody sees it coming.

**Pitch the person quoted in the announcement.** Their name is attached to the rollout publicly, which means the gap is theirs to explain internally.

Best timing is four to ten weeks after launch, when the gap has become somebody's problem rather than somebody's plan.

# Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **20 of the 38 company rows carry a named executive. The other 18 do not.** Funding press releases name CEOs reliably, so most of the missing ones are findable in about five minutes each. A row marked "Name not verified here" is unfinished research, not a dead end.

2. **No LinkedIn profile was opened.** The egress proxy blocks LinkedIn, so every name here came from a press release, a funding announcement or a company newsroom. Names are as published. Confirm the person is still in the seat before you write, because executives move.

3. **No email address appears anywhere, and none was constructed.** Unchanged rule. LinkedIn is the primary route for every row on this list.

4. **Where a row names only a function, that function is inferred from company stage rather than verified.** Where a row says "Marketing lead. Not named", that is a guess at the right function, not a confirmed org chart.

5. **Asia Pacific, Latin America and the Gulf are thin.** Singapore, Philippines, Hong Kong, Colombia, India, Saudi Arabia and the UAE appear once each. Competition for specialist deck work is far lower in those markets than in the US, so this is the most valuable gap on the list to close.

6. **Healthcare payers and provider systems are absent.** Payers buy an enormous amount of member-facing, broker-facing and employer-facing design. Not researched.

7. **Series A and B rounds from July and early August 2026 were not swept systematically.** The list skews to September because that is what surfaced. Companies that raised eight to ten weeks ago are arguably at the better moment, since the money has landed and the hiring has not.
